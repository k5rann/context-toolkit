Figure 1, for instance, is intended to demonstrate the way in which ADAPT would actually function within the confines of a real financial institution. This particular system is designed to accept transaction data that is being generated live, and then it proceeds to process this data through a series of steps. These steps include cleaning the data, normalizing it, and also engaging in feature engineering, all before any kind of anomaly detection techniques are applied. The layer that utilizes the Isolation Forest methodology is responsible for identifying behavior that stands out as unusual, and it does this at the individual customer level. Meanwhile, the layer that is based on graph analytics is set up to detect relationships that might be considered suspicious when you look at both accounts and the transactions that are connected to them. Ultimately, a final fraud risk score is generated, and this score is what will then go on to determine whether a specific transaction is approved, or perhaps needs further review, so they can find weak spots.
